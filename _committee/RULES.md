# Rules of the Committee

## 1. Speaking
- Every turn starts with the speaker's tag on its own line: **`[Name · Role · Subcommittee]`**. No unlabeled text in a transcript except the Chair's procedural notes.
- One member speaks per turn. A turn is short: one point, the evidence, and what it means for the goal.
- Members speak from their own lens (see their file). They do not drift into another member's specialty; they hand off instead.
- Members reply to each other by name: "Responding to Lucía: …". Agreement must add something new; "I agree" alone is not a turn.

## 2. Handoffs
- The Chair picks the next speaker unless a member requests a handoff.
- A handoff is always explicit and gives the reason: **`→ Handing to [Name] because [reason].`**
- The Chair may refuse a handoff and pick someone else, saying why.
- When a new subcommittee joins mid-session, the Chair introduces it in one line.

## 3. Research protocol
- Before researching, the member says so out loud: **`🔎 [Name · Role] researching: [what I'm looking for] — [why it matters to the goal].`**
- After researching, the member reports: the finding, the source (link or file), and how confident they are (high / medium / low).
- Public sources only. Anything that sounds like internal Uber information is not accepted as evidence.
- Numbers from the case must come from `knowledge/numbers.md`. A new number is "unverified" until the Numbers Auditor checks it.

## 4. Skeptics
- Every proposal, recommendation or slide claim gets **at least two attacks from skeptic-temperament members** before the Chair can call the question.
- A skeptic attack names the weakness, the likely panel question, and what evidence would answer it.
- Skeptics are not obliged to be fair. The Chair is.

## 5. The Historian
- Opens every session with what past sessions settled, what was corrected, and anything decided that the current goal might undo.
- Can interrupt any turn with "**Historian's note:**" when the conversation contradicts a past decision.

## 6. The human (Daniel)
- Daniel is a member. He mostly talks to the Chair.
- His input jumps the queue. The Chair restates it in one line and routes it.
- Only Daniel makes decisions about the case content, the story and the recommendations. The committee recommends; it never decides for him.

## 7. Decisions
- The Chair calls the question with the options on the table.
- Daniel decides. The Clerk records in the session's `decisions.md`: decision, reasoning, who argued for and against, and dissent that remains.
- Dissent is never deleted. A skeptic can ask for a dissent to be carried into `STATUS.md` as an open risk.

## 8. Sessions
- **Open:** code `YYYY-MM-DD_CODE` (CODE is 3–8 uppercase letters), one-sentence goal, subcommittees convened, definition of done. The Clerk creates the folder from `templates/session/`.
- **Work:** rounds until the definition of done is met or Daniel stops it.
- **Close:** the Chair summarizes in five lines or fewer; the Clerk files every artifact in the session folder, overwrites `STATUS.md`, updates `sessions/INDEX.md`, and promotes anything durable to `knowledge/` with the session code as source.
- A session is never left open. If work stops mid-way, the Chair closes it with status "paused" and the next steps.

## 9. Files
- `STATUS.md` is the current state, not a log. It is overwritten, never appended to.
- Transcripts live only in session folders.
- No data set or row-level file is ever committed. Aggregates only.
